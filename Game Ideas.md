# Game Ideas

Concept notes for a Chinese vocabulary game for Singapore primary students.
The current playable experiment is [Stormroot](https://goatedapps.github.io/RPG-ChineseGame2/), a P6 Lesson 1 rooftop platformer.
The [Lantern Detective Agency proposal](Lantern%20Detective%20Agency.html) is kept here as an earlier concept.

## Content and shared progression

| Level | Lessons | Vocabulary entries | Stories |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| P2 | 19 | 460 | 19 |
| P3 | 17 | 399 | 17 |
| P5 | 17 | 327 | 17 |
| P6 | 12 | 300 | 12 |

Vocabulary totals include words that recur across lessons.
The Tingxie list defines the required vocabulary for each lesson and includes pinyin, meanings, and example sentences.
Every listed word gets reading, meaning, and handwriting practice over the course of its lesson.
The stories are optional references for characters and events, and original stories can be written around the same vocabulary.
Give students regular opportunities to practise the question bank in the game and through a dedicated practice area.
Use tagged questions with the linked lessons and in later review.
Untagged questions are available for any lesson within their level.

Students can select P2, P3, P5, or P6 from the start.
Each level begins at Lesson 1 and saves its own progress.
Introduce a few words per mission, check recognition and meaning in context, then build from Hanzi Writer demonstrations to writing without an outline.
Use browser speech synthesis for Mandarin dictation with replay, and bring harder words back in later missions.
Save at clear stopping points so students can return when they want to.

## 1. Inkbound

A creature collecting adventure across islands made of ink and paper.
Choose companions, explore, solve encounters, and rescue creatures whose abilities open routes and reveal secrets.
Read signs and creature requests, write damaged inscriptions with Hanzi Writer, and listen to messages for dictation at communication devices.
The P3 Lesson 1 paper horse could become a rideable companion after its wheels are repaired.
Each lesson adds a region, and earlier vocabulary returns in companion missions.
The long term draw is building a favourite team and a sanctuary.
Companion choices and navigation should have consequences during exploration and encounters.

## 2. Cloudbuilders

A building game where students turn a floating island into a town.
Interpret resident requests, gather materials, place paths and buildings, then see how residents use what was built.
Read blueprints, listen to spoken deliveries, and write signs or labels with Hanzi Writer.
Each lesson opens a district, and older districts host review requests.
Town design is the long term draw.
Placement and connections should affect how the town works.

## 3. Lantern Detective Agency

A mystery adventure about missing objects, odd messages, and funny misunderstandings.
Inspect scenes, compare testimony, pin evidence, and choose an explanation.
Read clues for meaning, write damaged text with Hanzi Writer, and capture key words from spoken witness statements.
P5 Lesson 1, 《消失的“宝物”》, offers a ready-made case with a comic reveal.
Each lesson has one main case made up of short scenes and follow-up tasks that cover its full Tingxie list.
The agency's Practice Desk gives students replayable access to question bank exercises, including questions that work better as standalone practice.
The long term draw is finding out what happens next and building a casebook.
Let the student investigate in the opening scene and use the meaning of a clue to change where they look.

## 4. Skyrail Couriers

A delivery adventure across moving islands.
Choose a route, navigate obstacles, discover shortcuts, and upgrade a glider or rail board.
Read place signs and parcel instructions, listen to route messages, and write labels or repair markers with Hanzi Writer at safe stops.
Each lesson adds a delivery route, with earlier words returning in changed orders.
The long term draw is mastering routes and opening paths with new equipment.
Pause the action during handwriting and offer movement assistance.

## 5. Moonlight Market

A night market simulation where students choose what to sell, prepare stock, and build a stall that attracts regulars.
Read menus and notes, interpret customer requests, and write key words from orders using speech synthesis and Hanzi Writer.
P2 food and night market vocabulary and P6 cooking vocabulary fit naturally.
Each lesson adds a market evening or neighbourhood, and familiar customers bring review words back.
The long term draw is customising the stall and discovering who visits next.
Use clothing, books, events, and services alongside food to cover more of the curriculum.

## Research behind the concepts

[Ofcom’s 2026 gaming summary](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/grow-up-gaming-summary/growing-up-gaming-research-summary.pdf?v=426354) found that creative and building games were the most commonly played genre among UK children aged 3–17 who game on devices.
[Roy Morgan’s survey of Australian Roblox players aged 6–13](https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/10136-yas-roblox-for-young-australians-survey-december-2025) found that adventure, action, and simulation led the categories.
[Roblox’s 2025 search data](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/12/roblox-replay-decoded-search-style) highlighted role-play and simulation, including Brookhaven and Grow a Garden.
[Ofcom’s small 2025–26 qualitative panel](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/childrens-online-experiences/2026-insights-panel-wave-1-4/childrens-online-insight-panel-wave-1-4-report.pdf?v=418200) found interest in deeper strategy among some older participants.
These are signals from other markets and, in Roblox’s own data, a broader age range.
Interest and recall should be tested with the intended students.
