Lantern Detective Agency

Run a neighbourhood detective agency. Follow clues in Chinese, piece together what happened, and find the funny story hiding inside each mystery.

The student leads the investigation

A short case starts with something odd in the scene. The player decides what to inspect and whom to question. Reading a notice can send them to a new location. Listening to a witness can put a new clue on the board. Each case ends with the player picking an explanation and pinning the evidence that supports it.

  1. InspectTap an object or read a sign.
  2. InterviewChoose a question and listen.
  3. ConnectPin clues that fit together.
  4. ExplainShow how your evidence fits.

A wrong theory gets a specific lead: “Which clue tells us what was in the tin?” The player can reopen the scene with all their evidence intact. Solved cases become illustrated pages in a casebook, with the next mystery waiting on the agency board.

Sample mission · P5 Lesson 1

The missing “treasure”

At a school camp, Xiao Qiang says his treasure has vanished. The camp notice forbids valuables. The player needs to find the tin and work out what he meant by “treasure.” This example draws on the optional reference story 《消失的“宝物”》. The same lesson could use an original mystery built around its Tingxie words.

  1. Read the campsite notice

    Inspect the notice or interview Xiao Qiang first. The notice reads 禁止带贵重物品. Pin it as evidence of a rule and ask what made the missing item precious.

  2. Recover the route clue

    A damp note reads 请到海边＿＿. Play the witness’s line, 请到海边集合, and write 集合 in two Hanzi Writer squares. The restored note marks a place on the map and the player can search near the trees.

  3. Explain the discovery

    Inspect the tin and hear why its contents mattered to Xiao Qiang. Pick an explanation and pin a clue about the contents and a clue about who made them. The casebook records the reveal.

Try the notice clue

营地规定：禁止带贵重物品。

Reveal what was in the tin

It held a spare rib made by Xiao Qiang’s mum. The tin tells you what he was saving. His explanation tells you why he called it treasure.

Chinese helps the detective make progress

The Tingxie list is the curriculum. Give students a chance to read, understand, and write every listed word across the case’s scenes and follow-up tasks. Put words that fit the plot in clues and dialogue, and use short tasks such as filing a report to practise the rest.

Read and understand

Use the actual Tingxie list and sentence bank to author signs, notes, and dialogue. Ask the player to show they understand a word by choosing a lead, matching a clue to an object, or using evidence in a new situation.

Practise the strokes

At a quiet point in the case, Hanzi Writer demonstrates a character, then gives guided practice. Fade the outline as the player gains confidence. Show one square per character, and offer a stroke hint when a player is stuck.

Listen and write

Browser speechSynthesis reads a short Mandarin line that gives the target word context. Offer replay and slower playback. Begin with blank squares and keep the answer hidden until the player asks for a hint. If no usable Mandarin voice is available, offer text practice and leave the listening check pending.

Check recall later

Keep separate records for recognition, meaning in context, and writing from dictation. A hinted attempt still advances the scene and schedules another attempt. A later check with no outline or hints can count toward independent recall.

In the opening scene, students work on 禁止 and 贵重 through meaning, and 集合 through dictation. The rest of the case and its follow-up tasks cover all 18 entries in the P5 Lesson 1 list.

A place to practise the question bank

Use suitable bank questions as witness follow-ups, short evidence checks, or part of the case checkpoint. Give students an agency Practice Desk they can return to, with short sets and a choice of topics such as pinyin, word usage, cloze, and comprehension.

Tagged questions

Offer them with the lessons they link to. Bring them back in review after students have reached those lessons.

Untagged questions

Make them eligible in any lesson within the same primary level. Choose a mix that fits the activity, such as a short comprehension passage on the Practice Desk.

Show explanations where available and let students retry missed items. For longer written responses, let students attempt the question before revealing the model answer for comparison.

One case per lesson

Let students choose P2, P3, P5, or P6 on the agency board. Each level starts at Lesson 1 and saves progress separately. Each case can span several short sessions. To open the next case, solve the mystery, complete the practice for every Tingxie word, and finish a short checkpoint that checks reading, meaning, and writing. Save after each scene so students can stop mid-case.

P6 · 12 cases
Ask for a supported inference. In the bean-seed story, compare diary entries before and after the storm to explain what helped a seedling survive.

Track practice coverage across the full Tingxie list. Bring words that still need independent practice back in review, including in later cases when they fit.

What to test first

Start with the P5 campsite scene and a shorter P2 paper-bird scene. Give students a map, two or three things to examine, one dictation clue, and a final evidence choice. Aim for a five-to-eight-minute scene and adjust after observing both age groups.

Watch the investigation

Can students find a first clue without an adult directing them? Can they explain why their evidence supports their theory? At the checkpoint, offer a real choice to stop or open another lead.

Check what stuck

At a later session, ask students to interpret a new sentence and write one of the target words from audio without hints. Watch whether handwriting fits the pace and whether the Mandarin voice is clear on their devices.
